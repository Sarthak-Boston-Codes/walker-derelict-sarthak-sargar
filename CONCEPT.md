# CONCEPT.md — DERELICT

**The game in two sentences:** You play a lone scavenger exploring derelict, abandoned buildings — factories, houses, anything left to rot — after an experimental drug turned its test subjects into violent, infected husks. Each site is a tense search-and-clear: move carefully, avoid the infected where you can, and put them down when you can't.

## Core loop
Enter a derelict site → explore room by room, watching and listening for movement → encounter an infected, either slip past it or shoot it before it closes the distance → reach the extraction point to clear the site. Every encounter risks a hit if you're spotted or cornered; every shot fired risks drawing more infected toward the noise.

## Design pillars
1. **Every room could be empty, or could be it.** A handheld flashlight is the only reliable light source, so threats are genuinely hard to spot in advance, not telegraphed.
2. **Violence is a last resort, not a power fantasy.** The shoot action has a visible, deliberate wind-up (aim before fire) and an infected visibly takes a hit before going down — no instant one-shot kills that make the threat feel trivial.
3. **The world was abandoned for a reason.** Environment art leans into decay — rust, broken glass, collapsed shelving, plant growth indoors — over generic "warehouse" tiles, so exploration itself tells the story.
4. **Every failure teaches a route, not a punishment.** The hurt/fail state always makes clear which direction the threat came from, so a retry is about adjusting approach, not randomness.

## Art direction
Muted, desaturated palette — concrete grays, rust orange, sickly yellow-green — with one warm light source (the flashlight) cutting through near-total darkness everywhere else, so every lit area feels deliberate and every dark corner feels risky. Character and infected silhouettes stay simple and high-contrast against the dim environment so they read instantly even in low light.

Reference notes (materials/lighting/mood, not artists): a handheld flashlight cutting through a pitch-dark interior; rust and water-damage texture on concrete and metal; practical, modern gear rather than futuristic or fantasy equipment.

## Audio direction
Mostly near-silence, punctuated by small environmental sounds (dripping water, settling debris, a distant groan) so any real threat sound stands out immediately. Music stays sparse and tense during exploration — atmosphere more than melody — and should feel like it almost disappears the instant an infected is engaged, so the raw sound of the encounter itself (footsteps, the shot, the infected's noise) carries the moment.
